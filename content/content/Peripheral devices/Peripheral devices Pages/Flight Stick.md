# Flight Sticks

---

A **flight stick** is an input device designed to simulate the controls used to operate an aircraft. Flight sticks are commonly used with flight simulators, space simulators, and other games involving aircraft or spacecraft. Unlike a standard [[Controller]], a flight stick gives the player a larger physical control that can provide more precise movement when controlling pitch, roll, and other functions. Many flight sticks also include additional buttons, triggers, throttle controls, and programmable inputs that can be assigned to different actions within a game.

Flight sticks are a specialized type of [[Peripheral devices main pages|peripheral device]] and are not necessary for most PC games. However, they can provide a more immersive experience when playing games designed around flying.

---
## Flight Stick Controls

A typical flight stick uses the movement of the stick itself as its primary input. Moving the stick forward and backward can control the pitch of an aircraft, while moving it from side to side can control its roll. Some flight sticks can also be twisted to provide another axis of movement that can be used for functions such as controlling the aircraft's rudder.

Many flight sticks include additional buttons that can be assigned to functions normally controlled through a [[Keyboard]]. Depending on the device, these can include triggers, directional switches, throttle controls, and other programmable buttons.

## HOTAS Systems

A more advanced flight-control setup is commonly referred to as **HOTAS**, meaning **Hands On Throttle-And-Stick**. A HOTAS setup separates the flight stick and throttle into individual controls, allowing the player to control the aircraft without constantly moving their hands back to a [[Keyboard]] or [[Mouse]]. Companies such as **Thrust master** and **Logitech** manufacture flight-control systems designed for PC gaming. These devices range from relatively simple flight sticks to more advanced HOTAS systems with separate throttles and numerous programmable controls. ([[References]]) HOTAS systems can be particularly useful for flight and space simulation games because players may need access to many different controls while simultaneously controlling their aircraft.

---
## Flight Sticks and Immersive Gaming

Flight sticks can make certain games more immersive because the physical controls more closely resemble the controls being represented inside the game. Instead of controlling an aircraft entirely with a [[Keyboard]], [[Mouse]], or standard [[Controller]], the player physically moves a stick and throttle.
This is similar to how a Racing Wheel provides specialized controls for driving games. Both devices are designed around a particular type of game rather than being general-purpose input devices.
Flight sticks can also be combined with [[Virtual Reality]]. A VR headset can provide the visual experience of sitting inside a virtual cockpit while a flight stick provides physical controls for interacting with the aircraft.

---
## Flight Sticks and Multiplayer Gaming

Flight sticks can also be used while [[Playing games with friends main pages|Playing games with friends]]. Multiplayer flight and space simulation games can allow multiple players to fly together or compete against one another while using their own flight-control setups. Unlike local multiplayer games where several [[Controller|controllers]] might be connected to the same computer, multiplayer flight simulation is often played using separate computers connected through an online multiplayer game. Each player can use their own combination of a flight stick, throttle, [[Keyboard]], [[Mouse]], or other [[Peripheral devices main pages|Peripheral devices]]. The type of flight stick and the number of available controls needed will depend on the game being played. Some games can be played with a relatively simple joystick, while more complex simulators may benefit from a HOTAS system with additional programmable controls.
