# Virtual Reality
---
**Virtual reality (VR)** is a technology that allows a user to experience and interact with a computer-generated three-dimensional environment. Instead of viewing a video game entirely through a traditional monitor, a player wears a VR headset that displays images directly into their eyes and tracks the movement of their head. VR gaming can also use motion-tracked [[Controller|controllers]] that allow the player's hand movements to be represented within the virtual environment. This makes virtual reality one of the more immersive types of [[Peripheral devices main pages|peripheral devices]] available for gaming.

## Virtual Reality Headsets

A **VR headset** is the primary device used to experience virtual reality. The headset contains displays and sensors that allow the system to determine where the player is looking and adjust the displayed environment as the player's head moves. Popular VR systems include Meta's **Quest** headsets and Valve's **Index** system. Depending on the headset, VR games can either run directly on the headset or use the [[Hardware Main Page|hardware]] inside a gaming computer to render the virtual environment. ([[References]]) PC-based virtual reality can require relatively powerful [[Hardware Main Page|hardware]] because the computer must render the virtual environment while responding to the player's movements. Tis often is heavy computing for the [[GPU]].

## VR Controllers

Many virtual reality systems use specialized motion [[Controller|controllers]]. Unlike a traditional controller that primarily relies on buttons and analog sticks, VR controllers can track the position and movement of the player's hands. This allows games to represent physical actions inside the virtual environment. Depending on the game, a player may be able to reach for an object, aim a virtual weapon, operate equipment, or interact with menus using physical movements. VR controllers can still include traditional buttons, triggers, and analog sticks, allowing developers to combine physical motion with familiar controller inputs.

---
## VR and Computer Hardware

Virtual reality can place significant demands on computer hardware. A [[GPU]] is particularly important because it is responsible for rendering the images displayed inside the headset. The [[CPU]] and [[RAM]] also contribute to running the game and processing information required by the VR application. Because of these requirements, players should check the hardware specifications of a VR headset and game before attempting to use them with a computer. Some VR headsets can operate independently, while others rely heavily on a connected gaming PC. The [[Software]] used by the headset is also responsible for communicating between the VR hardware, controllers, computer, and game.

## VR and Other Peripheral Devices

Virtual reality can be combined with other [[Peripheral devices main pages|peripheral devices]] to create more specialized gaming experiences. For example, a [[Flight Stick]] can be used with a VR flight simulator. The player can visually experience sitting inside a virtual cockpit while physically controlling the aircraft using the flight stick. A Racing Wheel can provide a similar experience in racing simulators. The headset provides the visual environment while the wheel and pedals provide physical controls that more closely resemble driving an actual vehicle. These combinations demonstrate how multiple peripheral devices can work together to create a more immersive gaming experience.

---
## Virtual Reality With Friends

Virtual reality can also be used when [[Playing games with friends main pages|playing games with friends]]. Multiplayer VR games allow players in different locations to enter the same virtual environment and interact with each other. Depending on the game, players may communicate through Voice Chat and use their VR controllers to interact with other players. Multiplayer VR therefore combines several parts of a gaming system, including [[Software Main Pages|software]], computer hardware, networking, audio, and [[Peripheral devices main pages|peripheral devices]]. Some VR experiences can also involve people in the same physical location, although the person wearing the headset generally requires enough open space to safely move around while playing.
