# Steam

---

**Steam** is a digital game distribution platform developed by Valve. It allows PC players to purchase, download, install, update, and launch video games from a single application. Steam includes games from major publishers as well as independent developers and is one of the [[Game Launchers]] available for PC gaming. Steam also provides features beyond simply launching games. Players can maintain a friends list, communicate through text or voice chat, earn achievements, save supported game data to the cloud, and participate in the Steam Community. ([[References]])

## Steam Library

Games purchased or activated through Steam are added to the player's **Steam Library**. From the library, users can install and launch their games as well as access information such as achievements and community content. Steam also provides automatic game updates. This allows developers to distribute updates through Steam without requiring players to manually download each update from the developer's website. ([[References]]) Games installed through Steam still depend on the computer's [[Hardware Main Page|hardware]], [[Discord]], and [[Device Drivers]] to operate correctly.

---
Steam includes several features designed around [[Playing games with friends main page|playing games with friends]]. Players can add other Steam users as friends, join groups, communicate through Steam Chat, and use voice communication. Steam also provides game-specific community areas where players can participate in discussions and view content related to a particular game. ([[References]]) For games that support online play, Steam can be used alongside [[Multiplayer Games]], [[Dedicated Servers]], and other multiplayer technologies. The **Steam Workshop** allows users to create, discover, and download community-created content for supported games. Depending on the game, Workshop content can include maps, cosmetic items, additional content, or [[Game Mods|game modifications]]. ([[References]]) The exact way Workshop content works depends on the individual game. This makes Steam an example of how a [[Game Launchers|game launcher]] can provide additional services beyond purchasing and launching games.

## Controllers and Steam

Steam also supports games designed for different [[Peripheral devices main pages|peripheral devices]]. Valve specifically encourages developers to provide controller support, including support for Xbox, PlayStation, and Nintendo controllers. ([[References]]) This gives PC players the ability to choose between a [[Keyboard]] and [[Mouse]], a [[Controller]], or other supported input devices depending on the game they are playing.
